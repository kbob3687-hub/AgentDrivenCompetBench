Trello is a free product that works like the digital version of a sticky note. Capture your to-do's as cards on Trello and organize them into lists to stay on top of your tasks and deadlines no matter the undertaking—from planning a wedding to feeling organized at work. Trello is beloved by millions around the world for its easy-to-use, drag-and-drop interface that helps you visualize exactly what needs to get done.

Trello lets you take to-do’s from wherever you’re working and consolidate them all into one place:

📩 If you get an email you need to follow up on, forward it to [邮箱].com and it’ll turn into a Trello card that you can drag-and-drop to the right Trello board and add a due date.

🕸️ Download the Trello browser extension for Chrome to clip any aspect of a webpage as a Trello card.

💬 If you use apps like Slack, Teams, Jira or Loom in the workplace, Trello integrates with each to make it easy to capture content and centralize all your work tasks into a single unified view on Trello.

🤳 With Trello on your phone or tablet, you can use the app to create cards on Trello fast while on-the-go. Add a widget to your home screen on iOS or Android, make an “Add to Inbox” shortcut on iOS, or say “Siri, add a card to Trello.”

You’ll find everything saved as new cards in your Trello Inbox, ready to be edited and organized onto any board when you’re ready. Add due dates, comments, checklists, and files to individual cards and use colors, labels, images and stickers to create a beautiful kanban board tailored to your needs.

You can also add a Google or Outlook calendar account to easily see your entire schedule inside Trello. Drag-and-drop a Trello card onto the Trello Planner tab to block focus time on your calendar to get that task done.

"Trello Makes Workflow Tracking Effortless with Fast Onboarding and Smooth Integrations"

4.5/5

What do you like best about Trello?

Trello's board-and-card system makes it incredibly easy to visualize workflow stages at a glance, which has been especially useful for tracking cross-functional tasks across our logistics operations. The drag-and-drop interface requires almost no onboarding time, so new team members can start contributing within minutes rather than sitting through lengthy training sessions. Integration-wise, Trello connects smoothly with tools like Slack, Google Drive, and various automation platforms through Power-Ups, which lets us centralize notifications and file sharing without jumping between apps constantly. Performance has been consistently reliable even with dozens of boards and hundreds of cards running simultaneously, with minimal lag on both desktop and mobile. The built-in automation tool, Butler, adds a surprisingly capable layer of intelligence, letting us automate repetitive actions like due-date reminders, card sorting, and status updates without writing a single line of code. Review collected by and hosted on G2.com.

What do you dislike about Trello?

The pricing structure becomes a real consideration once you scale past a small team. Advanced features like unlimited Power-Ups, custom fields, and better reporting are locked behind the Business Class and Enterprise tiers, and the jump in cost per user adds up quickly for larger departments. Native reporting and analytics are fairly basic out of the box; anything beyond simple card counts requires third-party Power-Ups or exporting data elsewhere, which adds friction. For teams managing complex, interdependent projects with many moving parts, the flat card structure can feel limiting compared to tools with more robust dependency mapping or Gantt-style views. Support response times, particularly on lower-tier plans, can be slower than you'd like when you're dealing with a genuinely urgent issue. Review collected by and hosted on G2.com.

What problems is Trello solving and how is that benefiting you?

Trello has become our go-to tool for tracking operational tasks that don't need the overhead of a full project management suite — things like coordinating document reviews, tracking vendor follow-ups, and managing internal request queues.
