Notion CalendarNotion Calendar is a beautifully designed, standalone calendar application by Notion that brings together time management, scheduling, and task tracking in a single, unified interface. It is fully integrated with both Notion workspaces and Google Calendar, making it a powerful tool for individuals and teams who want to manage their work and personal schedules without switching between multiple apps.\\
\\
At its core, Notion Calendar allows users to connect multiple Google Calendar accounts and view all their events in one place. This makes it easy to manage both personal and professional calendars side by side, with a clean and intuitive interface available on desktop (macOS and Windows), web browsers, and mobile (iOS and Android).\\
\\
One of Notion Calendar's standout capabilities is its deep integration with Notion itself. Users can attach Notion pages directly to calendar events for easy access to relevant documents, notes, and project details. Additionally, Notion database dates — such as project deadlines, milestones, or task due dates — can be surfaced directly within Notion Calendar, eliminating the need to track important dates across separate tools. Users can even update Notion database dates directly from within the calendar view, keeping everything in sync without leaving the app.\\
\\
Notion Calendar also offers robust conferencing integrations. It supports Google Meet natively through its Google Calendar connection, and users can connect their Zoom accounts to automatically generate Zoom meeting links when creating new events. Beyond these, Notion Calendar supports custom conferencing connections, allowing users to add stable links for tools such as Microsoft Teams, Around, BlueJeans, Doxy.me, Skype, Webex, and Whereby. A default conferencing option can be set at the calendar level, so conferencing links are automatically added to new events with participants.\\
\\
For developers and power users, Notion Calendar exposes a local API that enables other tools to open Notion Calendar to a specific event, supporting deep linking workflows on macOS and Windows. This API accepts parameters such as account email, event identifier, start and end dates, and event title.\\
\\
Notion Calendar is available for free as part of the Notion ecosystem, making it accessible to users on all Notion plans. Whether used as a standalone scheduling tool or as a tightly integrated companion to a Notion workspace, Notion Calendar is designed to unify time, scheduling, and tasks into one beautiful, cohesive experience — helping users stay organized and focused without the friction of juggling multiple calendar and productivity apps.](https://www.g2.com/products/notion/subproducts/notion-calendar/reviews) [![Notion Mail](https://images.g2crowd.com/uploads/product/image/23f1df4a48002425b3574347f0ea5676/notion.png)\\

Notion MailNotion Mail is an email product offered by Notion as part of its broader AI workspace suite, available at www.notion.so. It is listed alongside other Notion sub-products such as Notion Calendar, Notion AI, and Notion Docs, indicating it is a dedicated email client or mail management tool integrated within the Notion ecosystem.\\
\\
Notion Mail appears to be designed to bring email management into the same connected workspace where teams handle projects, documents, knowledge bases, and meetings. By situating mail within the Notion platform, users can work across their communications and productivity tools without switching between separate applications.\\
\\
As part of Notion's broader vision of a collaborative AI workspace built on company context, Notion Mail is likely designed to leverage Notion's AI capabilities — such as AI-assisted writing, summarization, and automation — directly within an email workflow. This would allow users to draft, organize, and act on emails with the same intelligence layer that powers other Notion AI features like AI Meeting Notes and Enterprise Search.\\
\\
Notion's platform emphasizes connections between apps and workflows, and Notion Mail fits into this philosophy by serving as a communication layer that can interact with the databases, pages, projects, and documents already living in a user's Notion workspace. This integration-first approach means that emails can be linked to relevant projects, tasks, or knowledge base entries, reducing context-switching and keeping work organized in one place.\\
\\
Notion Mail is part of a suite that includes tools for automating busywork (Agents), finding answers instantly (Enterprise Search), managing projects, centralizing knowledge, and connecting external apps. Within this ecosystem, Notion Mail serves as the email and communication pillar, rounding out Notion's ambition to be a single, unified workspace for all work-related activities.\\

Notion SitesNotion Sites is a built-in website publishing feature within the Notion workspace platform, enabling individuals and teams to turn any Notion page into a live, publicly accessible website — without writing a single line of code. Described as "the easiest way to get a website up and running," Notion Sites removes the need for dedicated web developers or third-party website builders by letting users publish directly from their existing Notion content.\\
\\
With Notion Sites, users can publish an unlimited number of pages to the web, claim a personalized notion.site subdomain, and enable search engine indexing so their content is discoverable on the web. The publishing workflow is streamlined to just a few clicks: open a page, navigate to the Share menu, select the Publish tab, and the site goes live instantly. As edits are made to the underlying Notion page, the published site updates automatically — keeping content always in sync without any manual re-publishing steps.\\
\\
For users on paid plans, Notion Sites unlocks a richer set of customization and optimization tools. These include site themes to control the visual look and feel, search engine optimization (SEO) controls such as custom link titles and meta descriptions, and integration with Google Analytics for tracking visitor behavior and engagement. Paid plan users can also purchase a custom domain add-on, allowing them to connect an existing domain they own (e.g., careers.my-domain.com or portfolio.my-domain.com) to their Notion Site, with support for up to 25 custom domains per workspace. When using a custom domain, Notion branding can be removed from the site entirely.\\
\\
Notion Sites supports a wide range of content types natively available in Notion pages — including text, images, videos, databases, code blocks, and embeds from hundreds of third-party services. If a published site contains a Notion database, visitors can toggle between database views and open individual records directly from the site. Subpages are published alongside parent pages by default, though permissions can be restricted to hide specific subpages from public view.\\
\\
Additional visitor-facing features include the ability to search within a site, duplicate a site as a Notion template into their own workspace, and embed the published site into external HTML pages via an embed code. Site owners can also configure custom URL slugs (up to 60 characters) for individual pages to create clean, readable URLs.\\
\\
Notion Sites is available to all Notion workspace members, with free plan users able to publish unlimited pages and claim a notion.site domain, while paid plan users gain access to advanced SEO, theming, Google Analytics integration, and custom domain support. The custom domain add-on is priced at $10/month ($8/month with annual billing) per domain, on top of the existing Notion plan cost.\\
\\
Common use cases for Notion Sites include company websites, team blogs, personal portfolios, resumes, product landing pages, company handbooks, and documentation hubs — all built and maintained directly within the Notion workspace.](https://www.g2.com/products/notion/subproducts/notion-sites/reviews)

"Powerful writing tool, Simple UI with Great Integrations and AI Features"

5/5

What do you like best about Notion?

Notion has a quite unique document structure and a very simple yet powerful UI. It is very easy to integrate it with other apps (like Slack, email, etc) or MCP AI applications. It includes AI features that help both writing pages, correcting your writing or do smart search.. Easy to publish your page as an static webpage or export it as PDF. Review collected by and hosted on G2.com.

What do you dislike about Notion?

When exporting to PDF, it does not support diagrams in mermaid. In other words, it is great it has native support for Mermaid diagrams, but bad that you cannot export them to PDF (it exports the code, not the rendered diagram). Review collected by and hosted on G2.com.

"Database Buttons and Rollups helped us turn RFCs into a clearer review workflow"

What do you like best about Notion?

With Databases, Relations, and Rollups, we can connect RFCs, projects, tasks, incidents, and technical documentation without having to copy the same context into multiple places. A task can point back to its project, a decision can link to the RFC that explains it, and rollups can surface status or other aggregated information without needing to open every related record. This saves a lot of time when someone needs to understand why a decision was made months later. We also rely on Database Automations, Buttons, and Webhook Actions for smaller workflows. For example, a status change can assign a task, notify Slack, or send an HTTP POST to an external service—without another script running somewhere in the background. One unexpected benefit has been removing several tiny integrations that existed only to move information between a database and another tool. For anything more complex, we still use the Notion API with granular internal integrations and connection webhooks. Review collected by and hosted on G2.com.

What do you dislike about Notion?

Notion’s automation layer has some limits that start to show once a workflow becomes more complex. For example, one automation can’t directly trigger another. That does help prevent accidental loops, but it also means certain processes have to be redesigned or handled outside of Notion. Over time, we’ve learned to keep our workspace focused on coordination and shared context, and then move heavier logic into other services where it’s easier to test and maintain. Review collected by and hosted on G2.com.

What problems is Notion solving and how is that benefiting you?

A refactor had been running for several weeks, and our technical decisions were scattered across pull requests, meetings, and chat. To pull everything together, we created an RFC database tied to Projects, Tasks, and Meeting Notes. Each RFC tracked an owner, status, affected service, and the implementation work linked to it. We added a rollup to show whether any actions were still open, used a Database Button to create the standard review tasks, and set up a Webhook Action to send status changes to our internal tracking service. The first version had far too many properties, and nobody was sure which ones actually mattered. We simplified the schema and kept only the fields that helped people review decisions in the moment or find them later. We also used the Notion API to automatically link certain deployments back to the correct RFC. Overall, we spent much less time reconstructing context, and we got a clearer view of how a technical decision moved into implementation—without turning the workspace into another over-engineered internal app. Review collected by and hosted on G2.com.
